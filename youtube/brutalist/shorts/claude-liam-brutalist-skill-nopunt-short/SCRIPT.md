## B00 — ASK (14.58 s)
Hola, this is Liam, in for Bear. Today we tear down the nopunt skill. It is
the anti-punt catalog — the single place a Brutalist author checks before
dropping any placeholder in a beat sheet. It answers one question. Can I draw
this, and how. Here is the mental model.

## B01 — BLUF (20.38 s)
The easy read is that nopunt is a linter — a rejection tool that flags bad
placeholders. It is not. The nopunt skill is a catalog. A routing table you
check before you fill a beat. It maps narration shape to a real component,
and it refuses the punt costumes that keep sneaking back in. Only a genuine
archival photograph is a legitimate hold. Everything else, the catalog animates.

## B04 — MECHANISM ACT 1 (27.96 s)
First design decision, and it is the whole rule. HOLD versus PUNT. HOLD
means the beat needs a genuine archival photograph — a real person, a real
place, a real document, a real event — or a judgment only Bear can make.
That is the only legitimate placeholder. Everything else is a PUNT. A slot
the system could draw itself but deferred. Every row in the catalog below
is animatable. So if a beat's content appears in the catalog and is left
unfilled, that is a bug — not a design choice, not a stylistic hold, not
a taste question. Author it.

## B05 — MECHANISM ACT 2 (36.46 s)
Second decision. The catalog. Every beat's narration has a shape, and the
catalog maps that shape to a form and to the tool that renders it. Six
families. Data and quantity — bars, stat cards, donuts, itemized lists.
Structure and relationship — grids, funnels, flow diagrams, stacks, gates.
Math and mechanism — equations built term by term, geometric transforms.
Enumerated concepts — FormB cards, one icon per named thing. Code and app
and interface — the real artifact, in the real skin. And text bookends —
FormA and the four fixed beats. If a beat's content lands in any of these
families, the tool is already picked. The author's job is just to route it.

## B06 — MECHANISM ACT 3 (32.13 s)
Third decision. The block-list. Nopunt names five punt costumes — the same
bug in different hats — and none of them is ever a valid fill for animatable
content. One — YOU dropping in a gen dash AI clip. Two — an unfilled
fill underscore slates or remotion underscore scenes slate left in the
master. Three — a FormA card whose narration names a visual, like the
routing diagram or a funnel. Four — DoodleScene or DoodleChart, permanently
cut from this register. Five — STILL source archive, drop a historical image,
for a concept or a dataset. Each of these maps to a row in the catalog above.
Draw it.

## B07 — MECHANISM ACT 4 (33.21 s)
Fourth decision, and it is the vocabulary every authoring loop reads. SHOW,
HOLD, or CARD. SHOW — a real skin surface, a Manim diagram, a Remotion
scene, or a legible artifact renders the beat's content, and its component
is named in the shot block. HOLD — a real archival photograph, or a
judgment only Bear can make, with a one dash line hold underscore reason
next to it. CARD — a few words with nothing to show, an act divider or an
aphorism, last resort. If a card's narration names a visual or makes a
factual claim, that is a PUNT, not a card. Anything outside these three is
a PUNT — a bug, not a style choice.
